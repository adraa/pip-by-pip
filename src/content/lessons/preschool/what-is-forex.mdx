You have already traded forex. If you have ever landed in another country and handed over your money at an exchange counter, you did a forex trade. You gave up one currency, you received another, and the rate on the board decided how much you got.

That is genuinely all forex is. The professional version just involves much larger sums, much faster, and no queue.

<Analogy>
At the airport counter, a sign says the euro costs 1.09 dollars. You hand over 100 dollars and walk away with about 92 euros. If you come back next week and the euro costs 1.05 dollars, your 92 euros would buy back more than 100 dollars. You would have made money simply by holding euros while they got more expensive.

That is a forex trade. Everything else in this course is detail on top of that idea.
</Analogy>

## Currencies always come in pairs

Here is the first thing that trips people up. You cannot buy "the euro" on its own, because a price has to be measured in something.

Ask "how much is a euro?" and the only sensible answer is "in terms of what?" A euro costs about 1.08 US dollars, or about 0.86 British pounds, or about 165 Japanese yen. Three different prices for the same currency, all correct at the same time.

So currencies are always quoted two at a time.

Read it out loud as a sentence: **one euro costs 1.0850 US dollars**. When that number rises to 1.0900, the euro has got more expensive. When it falls to 1.0800, the euro has got cheaper and the dollar has got stronger.

<Callout>
Every price you will ever see on a forex chart is answering the same question: how much of the second currency does one unit of the first currency cost?
</Callout>

## It is enormous

Forex is not a niche corner of finance. It is the largest financial market in the world, by a distance that is hard to picture.

Size matters to you for one practical reason: **you can almost always get in and out**. In a small market, wanting to sell when nobody wants to buy is a real problem. In forex, at any normal hour, there is someone on the other side.

## There is no building

Ask where the stock market is and someone can point at a building. Ask where the forex market is and there is no answer, because it does not sit anywhere.

Forex is **over-the-counter**. It is a network: banks quoting prices to each other, brokers quoting prices to customers, all connected by screens. No trading floor, no bell, no single official price.

<Callout kind="warning" title="One consequence to remember">
Because there is no central exchange, there is no single official price. Two brokers can show you slightly different prices for the same pair at the same moment. They are usually close, but they are not identical.
</Callout>

## Why anyone bothers

Most of that 7.5 trillion has nothing to do with trading for profit. A German company selling machinery to America gets paid in dollars and needs euros to pay its staff. A tourist needs baht. A pension fund buying Japanese shares needs yen first.

All that ordinary business creates constant demand to swap currencies. Traders like us are a small passenger on a very large ship, trying to profit from where it drifts.

That is the whole picture. Money, swapped for other money, endlessly, all over the world. From here we start filling in how you actually do it.
