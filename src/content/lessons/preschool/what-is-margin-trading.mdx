This section is the most important in Preschool. Skip it and you will eventually destroy an account without understanding what happened.

## What margin actually is

Margin is **a deposit**, not a fee. Your broker sets it aside while your trade is open and gives it back, in full, when you close. It exists so that if the trade goes against you, there is money there to cover the loss.

<Formula expression="Required margin = Position size ÷ Leverage" note="At 100:1 leverage, a $100,000 position requires $1,000 of margin." />

<Analogy>
You rent a car worth 20 lakh. The rental company does not ask for 20 lakh. They put a 20,000 rupee hold on your card.

That hold is not payment. It is security. Return the car undamaged and it is released. Crash it and they take what they need.

Margin is that hold, and the crash is a losing trade.
</Analogy>

## What it lets you do

<Worked title="A $1,000 account at 100:1">
Without leverage, $1,000 buys about **920 euros**. A 50 pip move would earn you about **$5**. Not worth the effort.

With 100:1 leverage, your $1,000 controls **$100,000** of currency, one standard lot. Now a 50 pip move is worth **$500**.

That is why the whole industry runs on margin. Without it, small accounts could not meaningfully trade at all.
</Worked>

## And what it does to you

Read that example again, and notice it never said which direction.

<Callout kind="warning" title="The part that ends accounts">
Your losses are calculated on the **whole position**, not on your deposit.

Control $100,000 with $1,000, and the currency moves 1% against you. That is $1,000. Your entire deposit, gone, from a move that happens on an ordinary weekday afternoon.
</Callout>
