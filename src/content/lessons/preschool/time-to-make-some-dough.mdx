You now know how a trade makes money. The obvious next question is why the price moves at all.

The short answer: **money goes where it is treated best.** Everything else is detail on top of that.

## Interest rates are the engine

Every currency has a central bank, and every central bank sets an interest rate. That rate is roughly what your money earns just for sitting in that currency.

<Analogy>
Two banks on the same street. One pays 2% on your savings, the other pays 5%. Nothing else differs. Where do you put your money?

Now imagine the whole world making that decision, with trillions, continuously. That is the forex market.
</Analogy>

Higher rate, more attractive currency, money flows in, currency strengthens. Lower rate, money leaves, currency weakens. That single mechanism explains more currency movement than every chart pattern in this course put together.

## What matters is the surprise, not the news

Here is the part that makes beginners furious. A central bank raises rates, which should be good for the currency, and the currency **falls**. How?

Because the market had already expected the rise, and had already bought the currency in anticipation. By the time it happened, there was nobody left to buy, and some traders took their profit. The news was true and the price still went the other way.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Remember this one">
Prices move on the gap between what happened and what was expected to happen. Not on whether the news was good or bad.
</Callout>

Rates lead, but they do not act alone.
