## Why you should care

Size is not a bragging point. It has a direct, daily effect on you, and it has a name: **liquidity**.

Liquidity is the answer to "if I want out right now, is anyone there to take the other side?" In forex, at any reasonable hour, the answer is yes, instantly, at a price a hair away from the one on your screen.

<Analogy>
Try to sell your car at 3am in a village and you will wait, then take whatever the one interested buyer offers. Try to sell a hundred rupees' worth of a major currency at 3pm in London and it is gone before you finish clicking, at the price you saw.

Same asset, wildly different experience, entirely because of how many people are in the room.
</Analogy>

This is also why your trade size does not matter. A trader moving ten thousand dollars is invisible against seven and a half trillion. The market does not notice you, which is exactly what you want.

## Liquidity is not constant

It rises and falls through the day, following the working hours of the big financial centres.

<Callout kind="warning" title="The quiet hours cost more">
When the big desks are asleep, the gap between buying and selling prices widens, sometimes dramatically. The same trade can cost several times as much at 3am London time as it does at 1pm. Christmas Day, New Year and major holidays are worse still.
</Callout>

You will get the full map of this in the "When can you trade forex" section. For now, hold onto the shape of it: **midday London through to midday New York is the deep water, and everything else is shallower.**

## What size does not give you

Being enormous makes forex easy to enter and exit. It does not make it easy to predict, and it does not make it safe. A liquid market will happily let you lose money extremely efficiently.

Liquidity is a feature of the plumbing, not a promise about your results.
