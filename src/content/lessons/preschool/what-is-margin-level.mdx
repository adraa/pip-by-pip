This is the number that decides whether your positions stay open. It is worth understanding properly.

<Formula expression="Margin level = (Equity ÷ Used margin) × 100" note="Higher is safer. Falling toward 100% is trouble." />

## Reading it

<Worked title="The same used margin, three situations">
Used margin is **$500** throughout.

**Equity $2,500:** margin level **500%**. Very comfortable.
**Equity $1,000:** margin level **200%**. Fine.
**Equity $500:** margin level **100%**. Margin call. Your equity has fallen to exactly the locked margin.
**Equity $250:** margin level **50%**. Stop out. The broker closes your trades.
</Worked>

<Analogy>
Think of it as the fuel gauge, not the fuel tank.

Used margin is how far you have to drive. Equity is how much fuel you have. Margin level is the ratio of the two: how comfortably you can complete the journey.

At 100% you have exactly enough and no room for a detour. At 50% you are already coasting.
</Analogy>

## The two lines to know

Almost every broker sets two thresholds:

- **Margin call level**, commonly **100%**. A warning. No new trades allowed, and often an email or an alert.
- **Stop out level**, commonly **50%**. The broker begins closing your positions, whether you like it or not.

Both numbers vary between brokers, which is a lesson of its own further along. Check yours.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Nobody asks your permission">
At the stop out level your broker closes your trades automatically. There is no phone call, no confirmation, no chance to explain that it is about to turn around.

They are protecting themselves from your account going negative and owing them money. That is the entire arrangement, and you agreed to it when you opened the account.
</Callout>

## Keeping it high

There are only two ways to raise your margin level, and one of them is not available in a crisis.

1. **Increase equity** — deposit more money, or have your trades move in your favour.
2. **Reduce used margin** — close a position, which frees its deposit.

<Callout>
Notice that when your account is in trouble, depositing more is the emotional choice and closing a position is the sane one. Traders who deposit into a losing position are not solving the problem. They are buying it more time to get worse.
</Callout>
