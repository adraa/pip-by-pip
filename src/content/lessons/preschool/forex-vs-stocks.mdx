They are both markets with charts, and they are genuinely different things.

## The difference that matters

A share is **a piece of a business**. If the business grows, the share can grow with it, for years, and everyone who holds it can win at the same time because real value was created.

A currency pair is **a ratio between two countries' money**. If the euro strengthens, the dollar weakened by exactly the same amount. Nothing was created. Someone gained precisely what someone else gave up.

<Analogy>
Buying shares is like planting an orchard. The trees can grow, and if they do, everyone with a tree is better off.

Trading forex is like a tug of war. The rope does not get longer. It just moves, and the movement is the whole game.
</Analogy>

That single distinction produces most of the practical differences below.

## Side by side

| | Forex | Shares |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hours | 24 hours, 5 days | Local exchange hours |
| Choices | About 8 currencies, a couple of dozen useful pairs | Thousands of companies |
| Short selling | As easy as buying | Needs borrowing, fees and rules |
| Costs | Spread, sometimes a small commission | Commission, sometimes stamp duty and exchange fees |
| Paid to hold | No, and usually a small overnight cost | Often, via dividends |
| Long-term growth | No underlying growth; pairs oscillate | A good company can compound for decades |
| Typical leverage | High, and dangerous | Low or none for retail |
| What drives it | Interest rates, economies, central banks | Company earnings, products, management |

<Callout>
These are not rivals. Plenty of people invest in shares for the long term and trade forex separately with a small, deliberately limited amount of money. That is arguably the most sensible arrangement of the two.
</Callout>

## The trap to avoid

Do not treat forex like share investing. "It has fallen a long way, so it must be cheap" is a reasonable thought about a solid company. It is a dangerous thought about a currency, which has no floor made of assets, earnings or anything else, and which is perfectly capable of falling for another year.

Buy and hold is an investing strategy. Forex is not investing.
