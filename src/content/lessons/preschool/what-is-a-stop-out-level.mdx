The margin call was a warning. The stop out is not.

<Formula expression="Stop out level = the margin level % at which the broker closes your trades for you" note="Commonly 50%, but it varies widely by broker." />

## What happens, in order

<Steps
  steps={[
    { title: 'Your margin level reaches the stop out', body: 'Commonly 50%. Your equity is now half the margin locked up.' },
    { title: 'The broker closes a position', body: 'Usually the largest losing one, since that releases the most margin.' },
    { title: 'It recalculates', body: 'Closing one trade frees its margin and realises its loss, producing a new margin level.' },
    { title: 'If still below, it closes another', body: 'And repeats, until the margin level is back above the threshold or nothing is left.' },
  ]}
/>

<Worked title="A stop out in progress">
Three trades open, used margin **$600**, equity has fallen to **$300**.

Margin level = (300 ÷ 600) × 100 = **50%**. Stop out triggered.

The broker closes the largest loser. That trade's **$250** of margin is released and its **$180** loss is realised.

New used margin: **$350**. New equity: **$300**.
New margin level = (300 ÷ 350) × 100 = **86%**.

Above 50%, so the closures stop. You keep two positions and a much smaller account.
</Worked>

<Callout kind="warning" title="The cruellest detail">
Stop outs happen at the worst possible moment, by definition: the instant the market has moved furthest against you. That is very often the exact point at which it turns around.

Traders watch their positions closed at the low, then watch the price recover an hour later. The stop out did not cause that. Position size did.
</Callout>

## When it happens without warning

Both thresholds are checked continuously, but in a genuinely fast market, or across a weekend gap, the price can jump straight through both. You get no warning, no margin call, just a closed account and an email.

This is precisely why "I will watch it closely" is not a risk management plan.

## Can you end up owing money?

This depends entirely on where your broker is regulated.
