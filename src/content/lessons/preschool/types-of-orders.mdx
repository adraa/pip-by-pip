You cannot watch a chart all day, and you should not try. Orders are how you tell your broker what to do while you are asleep, at work, or making dinner.

There are three that matter, and everything else is a variation.

## When you would use each

<Worked title="EUR/USD is trading at 1.0850">
**You think it is going up and you want in now.**
Market order. You are filled around 1.0851 immediately.

**You think it is going up, but 1.0850 is expensive. You would happily buy a dip.**
Buy limit at 1.0800. If the price dips there, you are in at your price. If it never dips, you never trade.

**You think it only really takes off if it clears 1.0900.**
Buy stop at 1.0905. Nothing happens unless the price climbs through that level. This is a breakout trade.

**You are already long and you are willing to lose 40 pips before admitting you were wrong.**
Stop loss at 1.0810. If it gets there, you are out automatically, and the loss stops being your decision.
</Worked>

## The two attached to every trade

Once you are in a position, two orders should already be waiting.

- **Stop loss** — closes you out if the price goes against you by a set amount. The most important order in trading, and the one beginners skip.
- **Take profit** — closes you out at your target. Less critical, but it stops greed from undoing a good trade.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Set the stop when you enter, not later">
"I will watch it and close it manually if it goes wrong" is what everyone says, and it fails in exactly the moment it matters. When a trade is 60 pips against you, your brain will invent reasons to wait. A stop loss placed while you were still calm does not negotiate.
</Callout>

## Variations you will meet

- **Trailing stop** — a stop loss that follows the price when the trade is winning and stays put when it turns. Locks in profit without your attention.
- **Good till cancelled (GTC)** — the order waits indefinitely until you remove it.
- **Good for the day (GFD)** — it expires at the end of the trading day.
- **One cancels the other (OCO)** — two orders, and whichever triggers first kills the other. Useful when you would trade a breakout in either direction.
- **Fill or kill** — fill the whole order right now or cancel it entirely.

You do not need most of these. You need market, limit, stop and stop loss, and you need them to be automatic.
