"Open 24 hours" is true and slightly misleading. The market is technically available all night, in the same sense that a motorway is available at 4am. Available is not the same as busy.

## Why it never sleeps

There is no building to lock up. Trading simply follows the working day around the planet. Sydney opens, then Tokyo, then London, then New York, and by the time New York goes home Sydney is waking up again.

Play with that for a moment before reading on. Two things are worth noticing: the market is never truly empty, and it is only genuinely crowded twice.

## The four sessions

| Session   | GMT hours    | Character                                              |
| --------- | ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Sydney    | 21:00 – 06:00 | Quietest. The week begins here on Sunday evening.       |
| Tokyo     | 00:00 – 09:00 | Steady. Often range-bound. AUD, NZD and JPY pairs move. |
| London    | 07:00 – 16:00 | The biggest. Trends often start here.                   |
| New York  | 12:00 – 21:00 | Busy for its first half, then fades badly.              |

<Callout>
Those hours shift by an hour twice a year, because different countries change their clocks on different dates. If your platform time is unfamiliar, check what timezone it uses. Most brokers use GMT+2 or GMT+3, not your local time.
</Callout>

## The two overlaps

This is the part that actually matters for planning your day.

- **Tokyo and London, 07:00 to 09:00 GMT.** A modest overlap. Europe arrives while Asia finishes, and the day's direction often gets decided here.
- **London and New York, 12:00 to 16:00 GMT.** The main event. The two largest centres are open together, volume peaks, spreads are at their tightest, and the largest moves of the day usually happen in this window.

If you only have a couple of hours a day to trade, that second window is almost certainly the one to pick.

## And the dead zone

Between about 21:00 and 00:00 GMT, New York has gone home and Tokyo has barely arrived. Volume collapses, spreads widen, and prices drift aimlessly.

<Callout kind="warning">
Trading the dead hours costs you more, because spreads are wider, and gives you less, because prices are not really going anywhere. If those are the only hours you have free, trade a longer timeframe and place orders rather than sitting and watching.
</Callout>

## The weekend

From Friday evening to Sunday evening the market genuinely closes. That matters for one reason: news happens over the weekend, and Monday's opening price can be well away from Friday's closing price. That jump is called a **gap**, and a stop loss cannot protect you across it.

Holding trades over a weekend is a real decision, not a default.
