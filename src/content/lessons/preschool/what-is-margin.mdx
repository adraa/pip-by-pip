Every trade you open needs its own deposit. That deposit is the **required margin**, and it is worked out from two things: how big the trade is, and how much leverage you have.

<Formula expression="Required margin = Position size ÷ Leverage" />

## Working it out

<Worked title="Three trades, same account">
Leverage of **100:1** throughout, EUR/USD at 1.0850.

**One micro lot** (1,000 euros, about $1,085)
$1,085 ÷ 100 = **$10.85 of margin**

**One mini lot** (10,000 euros, about $10,850)
$10,850 ÷ 100 = **$108.50 of margin**

**One standard lot** (100,000 euros, about $108,500)
$108,500 ÷ 100 = **$1,085 of margin**
</Worked>

Notice that the margin is worked out from the value of the position in your **account's currency**, which is why the current exchange rate creeps into the sum.

## Margin requirement is the same thing, said backwards

Some brokers quote a percentage rather than a ratio. They mean the same thing.

| Margin requirement | Leverage | $10,000 position needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 50% | 2:1 | $5,000 |
| 10% | 10:1 | $1,000 |
| 3.33% | 30:1 | $333 |
| 2% | 50:1 | $200 |
| 1% | 100:1 | $100 |
| 0.2% | 500:1 | $20 |

<Formula expression="Leverage = 100 ÷ Margin requirement %" note="A 2% requirement is 50:1. A 0.5% requirement is 200:1." />

## It is a deposit, not a cost

Worth repeating because it genuinely confuses people: **margin is not spent.** It is set aside. Close the trade and every cent of it comes straight back, whatever the trade's result.

What you lose or gain is a separate matter, calculated from the price move.

<Callout kind="warning" title="The regulated leverage difference">
Where you live changes this dramatically. In Europe, the UK and Australia, retail leverage on major pairs is capped at **30:1**, so a standard lot needs about $3,600 of margin rather than $1,085.

Offshore brokers advertising 500:1 are not offering you something better. They are offering you enough rope, in a jurisdiction where nobody will help you if it goes wrong.
</Callout>

## Several trades at once

Each open trade locks its own margin. Three mini lots open at once at 100:1 lock roughly $325 in total, and that is money no longer available to absorb losses.

Add them all together and you get **used margin**, which is coming in two lessons' time.
