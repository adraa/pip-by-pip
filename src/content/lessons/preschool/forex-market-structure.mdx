There is no forex exchange. No building, no bell, no single official price. Instead there is a **ladder**, and where you stand on it decides what price you see.

<Analogy>
Think of vegetables. The wholesaler buys a tonne at the farm gate for the lowest price anyone will ever pay. A distributor buys from the wholesaler for a bit more. The corner shop buys from the distributor. You buy from the corner shop.

Same vegetables, four prices, and each step is somebody's living. Forex is that, with currency, and with much thinner margins.
</Analogy>

## Why nobody built an exchange

Currencies are not issued by one authority and are traded by every government, bank and company on earth, continuously, around the clock. There was never a natural place to put an exchange, and the market grew as a network of relationships instead.

This has consequences you will meet every week:

- **No official price.** Two brokers can show slightly different numbers for the same pair at the same moment, and neither is wrong.
- **No official volume.** When an indicator shows you forex "volume", it is your broker's volume, or futures volume used as a proxy. It is not the market's.
- **Nobody can corner it.** No single institution is big enough to control the price of a major currency for long. Even central banks manage it only briefly.

<Callout kind="warning">
Neither model is automatically evil, but the second one has a conflict of interest baked into it. That is why "is this broker properly regulated?" is the most important question in Kindergarten, and why it deserves more of your attention than choosing an indicator ever will.
</Callout>

## What this means for you

You are at the bottom of the ladder. You cannot change that, and it does not stop you being profitable. It just means your costs are structural, not negotiable, and the only lever you control is choosing a broker who does not add more markup than they need to.
