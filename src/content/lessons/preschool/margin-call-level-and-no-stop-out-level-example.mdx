Some brokers set the margin call and the stop out at the same number, typically 100%. This lesson walks through exactly what that feels like, minute by minute.

## The setup

<Worked title="The account">
Deposit: **$1,000**
Leverage: **100:1**
Broker: margin call level **100%**, no separate stop out. Closure happens at 100%.

The trade: buy **1 mini lot** of EUR/USD at **1.0850**. One pip is worth **$1**.

Required margin = $10,850 ÷ 100 = **$108.50**
</Worked>

## As the price falls

| Price | Pips against | Floating P/L | Equity | Margin level | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.0850 | 0 | $0 | $1,000.00 | 922% | Comfortable |
| 1.0750 | -100 | -$100 | $900.00 | 830% | Fine |
| 1.0550 | -300 | -$300 | $700.00 | 645% | Uncomfortable |
| 1.0250 | -600 | -$600 | $400.00 | 369% | Worrying |
| 0.9959 | -891 | -$891 | $109.00 | 100% | **Closed** |

The trade survives an enormous 891 pip move, then ends abruptly.

<Callout>
Notice what killed it. Not a chart pattern, not bad analysis. It ran out of equity relative to the locked margin. The price only had to keep going.
</Callout>

## The bit that stings

At the moment of closure your equity is **$109**. You deposited $1,000. You have lost **89% of your account on a single trade**, and you never made a decision about it. The system did.

## The same thing with a stop loss

<Worked title="One line of difference">
Identical account, identical trade, but with a stop loss at **1.0750**, 100 pips away.

The price falls. At 1.0750 the stop triggers and the trade closes.

Loss: **$100**. Remaining balance: **$900**. Margin level: back to infinity, because nothing is open.

You are down 10% and entirely intact. You get to trade tomorrow, and the day after.
</Worked>

The difference between losing 10% and losing 89% was one order, placed at the beginning, when you were calm.

## Why this setup is the harsher one

<Callout kind="warning">
A broker with a 100% margin call and a separate 50% stop out gives you a zone: a warning, then some room, then the closure. You can act in between.

A broker with them combined gives you nothing. The warning and the execution are the same event.
</Callout>

Neither arrangement is wrong, and both are common. Find out which one you have **before** you need to know, because the difference decides how much warning you get.
