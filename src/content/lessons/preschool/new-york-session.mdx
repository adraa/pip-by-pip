New York is a session of two halves, and the difference between them is stark.

<SessionsClock caption="Drag from 13:00 to 19:00. Watch the moment London closes and New York is left on its own." />

## The first half: the best window of the day

From **12:00 to 16:00 GMT**, London and New York are open together. The world's two largest financial centres, at the same time.

- Volume peaks
- Spreads are at their tightest
- The largest moves of the day usually happen here
- Almost every major US economic release lands in this window

If you have a job and can only trade for a couple of hours, this is the window to fight for.

## Why American news moves everything

The dollar is on one side of the overwhelming majority of forex trades. So a US employment report does not just move the dollar; it repricies the entire board simultaneously.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Release times worth knowing">
Most US data lands at **12:30 or 14:00 GMT**. Federal Reserve decisions usually come at **18:00 or 19:00 GMT**.

In the seconds around a big release, spreads can widen tenfold and the price can jump straight past your stop loss. Beginners should be flat before major releases, not clever.
</Callout>

## The second half: not much

Once London closes at 16:00 GMT, roughly two thirds of the day's volume simply leaves.

The late session has a habit of drifting, and sometimes of quietly unwinding the day's move on almost no volume, which is maddening if you are still holding.

## What this means for your day

- Aim for **12:00 to 16:00 GMT** if you possibly can.
- Check the calendar for US releases before entering anything.
- Treat the last two hours of New York with suspicion. Thin markets do strange things.
- If you hold a trade past 21:00 GMT, you are holding it into the quietest hours of the day. Decide that deliberately.
