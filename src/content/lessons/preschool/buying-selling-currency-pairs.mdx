Every pair has a first currency and a second one, and the order matters enormously. The first one is the **base**. Everything the price says is said about the base.

## Two directions, both open to you

In most markets you make money when things go up. Forex has no natural "up", because a price is a ratio between two currencies. So both directions are equally available, and equally normal.

Selling something you do not own sounds strange, and in most markets it is a complicated manoeuvre. In forex it is not, because a sale of one currency is automatically a purchase of the other. Selling EUR/USD just means you are holding dollars instead of euros. Nothing exotic is happening.

<Analogy>
You are at the airport and you think the pound is about to fall. So you swap your pounds for dollars now. A week later the pound has fallen, you swap back, and you end up with more pounds than you started with.

You just went short the pound. No borrowing, no tricks. You simply held the other currency for a while.
</Analogy>

## Say it out loud

The confusion always comes from forgetting which currency you are talking about. Get in the habit of reading the trade as a full sentence.

<Steps
  steps={[
    { title: 'Buy EUR/USD', body: 'I am buying euros and paying with dollars. I want euros to get more expensive.' },
    { title: 'Sell EUR/USD', body: 'I am selling euros and taking dollars. I want euros to get cheaper.' },
    { title: 'Buy USD/JPY', body: 'I am buying dollars and paying with yen. I want the dollar to get more expensive.' },
    { title: 'Sell USD/JPY', body: 'I am selling dollars and taking yen. I want the dollar to get cheaper.' },
  ]}
/>

<Callout kind="warning">
The most common beginner mistake is thinking "I want the dollar to go up" and then buying EUR/USD. That trade wants the dollar to go **down**. Always check which side of the slash your opinion is on.
</Callout>

## The price only ever tells you one thing

Whatever chart you are staring at, the number answers a single question: how many units of the second currency does one unit of the first cost?

Rising number, base getting stronger. Falling number, base getting weaker. That is the entire language.
