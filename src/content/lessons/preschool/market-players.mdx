## The important thing about that list

Look again at groups two, three and four. **Most of that money is not trying to beat the market.**

<Analogy>
A tourist changing money at the airport is not trying to outsmart anyone. They need rupees, they have dollars, they accept the rate on the board and get on with their holiday.

Now scale that up to a company paying a hundred million dollars for machinery. Same indifference to price, vastly larger transaction.
</Analogy>

That indifferent flow is the ocean everyone else swims in. It is why forex is so deep and so liquid, and it is why you are not, in fact, competing directly against every trader on earth for every pip. A great deal of the volume simply is not playing your game.

<Callout>
Where a stock market is largely a room full of people trying to outsmart each other, forex is largely plumbing, with speculators in the pipes.
</Callout>

## The one group who can flatten you

Central banks are small in volume and huge in consequence.

They move the market in two ways. Usually by setting interest rates, which is slow, scheduled and analysable. Occasionally by **intervention**: stepping into the market and trading their own currency deliberately to force the price.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Intervention is brutal">
The Swiss National Bank abandoned its cap on the franc in January 2015. EUR/CHF fell nearly 30% in minutes. Stop losses filled hundreds of pips away from where they were set, brokers went bankrupt, and a great many accounts went negative.

It is rare. It is also completely outside your control, which is a good argument for never having so much on the table that one bad minute matters.
</Callout>

## Where you fit

You are small, fast and unconstrained. You have no client orders to fill, no mandate, no committee. You can sit out for a week if nothing looks good.

None of the big participants can do that. It is genuinely your only structural advantage, and most retail traders throw it away by trading constantly.
