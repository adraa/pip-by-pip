The simplest number on your platform, and the one that misleads people most often.

<Formula expression="Balance = Deposits − Withdrawals + Closed trade results" note="Open trades are not in this formula at all." />

## It only records finished business

Your balance is a record of things that have **already happened**. Money you put in, money you took out, and the results of trades you have closed.

An open trade, however large, however deep in profit or loss, is not part of it.

<Worked title="Watching the balance sit still">
You deposit **$1,000**. Balance: **$1,000**.

You open a trade. Balance: still **$1,000**. Opening a trade does not spend anything; it only locks some margin aside.

The trade goes 100 pips against you, showing minus $100 on screen. Balance: still **$1,000**.

It recovers and shows plus $200. Balance: still **$1,000**.

You close it at plus $150. Balance: **$1,150**. Only now.
</Worked>

## The trap

This is exactly where beginners get caught out.

<Callout kind="warning" title="A healthy balance means nothing">
Your balance can read $1,000 while your account is minutes from being wiped out, because a $900 floating loss does not appear in it.

The number that matters is **equity**, which counts the open trades. That is the next lesson but one, and it is the one to watch.
</Callout>

## The small print: swaps

One thing does quietly change your balance without you closing anything: **swap**, sometimes called rollover.

Hold a position past the daily rollover, usually 22:00 GMT, and you are holding one currency while owing another. The difference in their interest rates is credited or debited. It is usually pennies on a micro lot, and it accumulates on larger, longer trades.

<Callout>
Swaps are triple-charged on Wednesdays, to account for the weekend when the market is closed. If a trade suddenly costs three times as much to hold, that is why, and nothing has gone wrong.
</Callout>
