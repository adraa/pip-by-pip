If you can only trade one session, this is usually the one.

## Why it dominates

London handles a larger share of daily forex volume than any other centre, and it has for a very long time. Two reasons: history, since international currency dealing grew up there, and geography, since London's working day touches the end of Asia's and the start of America's.

The practical consequence is that **the market often does not really wake up until London does.** Prices that drifted sideways for six hours suddenly develop an opinion at 07:00 GMT.

## What moves

Europe is at work, so European currencies carry real business flow:

- **EUR/USD** — the most traded pair in the world, at its most active
- **GBP/USD** — busy and notably jumpy
- **EUR/GBP**, **EUR/CHF** and the other European crosses, which barely move at any other time

Spreads are at or near their tightest for the day, which quietly makes every trade cheaper.

## The opening hour

The first hour or two has its own reputation, and it is deserved.

<Callout kind="warning" title="The London open trap">
A large amount of pent-up volume arrives at once. The price often lurches hard in one direction, stops out everyone positioned the other way, then reverses and makes the real move.

Traders who arrive with a plan use this. Traders who arrive with excitement get taken apart by it.
</Callout>

Two sane approaches: either wait thirty to sixty minutes and trade once the first burst has resolved, or use wider stops that can survive the noise. Trading the first five minutes with a tight stop is how beginners donate money.

## The afternoon

From about **12:00 GMT** New York joins in, and the next four hours are the busiest of the trading day. That overlap gets its own lesson, because it is where a great deal of the week's real movement happens.

After New York's morning, London traders start going home, volume drains away, and the late afternoon often turns into aimless drift.
