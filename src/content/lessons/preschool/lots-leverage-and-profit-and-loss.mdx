A pip is a distance. A **lot** is how much you are carrying while you walk it.

## Three sizes

| Name     | Units   | One pip is worth | Reasonable for            |
| -------- | ------- | ---------------- | ------------------------- |
| Standard | 100,000 | about $10        | Large, experienced accounts |
| Mini     | 10,000  | about $1         | A funded, tested account   |
| Micro    | 1,000   | about $0.10      | Learning, and your first live trades |

Same market, same pips, entirely different consequences.

<Worked title="One 50 pip loss, three sizes">
The price goes 50 pips against you. You close the trade.

- **Standard lot:** down **$500**
- **Mini lot:** down **$50**
- **Micro lot:** down **$5**

Nothing about your analysis changed between those three lines. Only the size did.
</Worked>

## How you afford 100,000 of anything

Obvious problem: a standard lot means controlling a hundred thousand dollars' worth of currency, and you do not have that. This is where **leverage** comes in.

Your broker lets you put down a small deposit and control a much larger position. At 100:1 leverage, $1,000 of yours controls $100,000 of currency.

<Analogy>
You buy a house worth 50 lakh with 5 lakh of your own money and a 45 lakh loan. The house rises 10% and you have made 5 lakh, doubling your money. Wonderful.

Now the house falls 10%. Your entire 5 lakh is gone, and you still owe the bank.

That is leverage. It does not care which direction it is amplifying.
</Analogy>

## The dangerous part, seen properly

Leverage does not increase your profit *rate*. It increases your **size**, and size cuts both ways with perfect symmetry. Play with this until it feels uncomfortable, because that discomfort is the lesson.

<MarginDashboard balance={1000} leverage={100} lots={0.1} move={-30} caption="Push the trade size up with a $1,000 deposit and watch how few pips it takes to reach a margin call." />

Notice what happens if you set the trade size to 1 lot on a $1,000 deposit. It takes a move of well under a hundred pips, a perfectly ordinary Tuesday, to destroy the account.

<Callout kind="warning" title="The number that actually matters">
Beginners ask "how much leverage does this broker offer?" as though more is better. The question that matters is "how many lots am I trading relative to my account?" You can have 500:1 available and trade responsibly. You can have 20:1 and blow up.
</Callout>

## Where to start

Micro lots. One pip costs you ten cents, a bad day costs you the price of a coffee, and you learn exactly the same lessons you would learn losing hundreds.

There is no prize for being brave early.
