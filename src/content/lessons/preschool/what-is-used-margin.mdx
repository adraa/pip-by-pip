Used margin is the least complicated number in this section. Add up the deposit on every open trade.

<Formula expression="Used margin = Sum of required margin on all open positions" />

<Worked title="Three trades open at once">
Leverage 100:1, account in dollars.

| Trade | Size | Required margin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EUR/USD long | 1 mini lot | $108 |
| GBP/USD short | 2 mini lots | $253 |
| USD/JPY long | 1 mini lot | $100 |

**Used margin: $461.**

Close the GBP/USD trade and used margin drops immediately to **$208**. The $253 is released back to you the moment the position is gone.
</Worked>

## Why it matters

Used margin is not a cost. It is still your money. But while it is locked up, **it cannot do the one job that keeps you alive: absorbing losses.**

<Analogy>
You have 10,000 rupees in your wallet, but 8,000 of it is a security deposit you have promised to a landlord and cannot touch.

You are not poor, exactly. But you can only handle a 2,000 rupee emergency, and the moment something bigger happens you are in trouble, despite the 10,000 on paper.
</Analogy>

Use the sliders to watch this directly. Push the trade size up and notice used margin climbing while free margin shrinks toward nothing.

<MarginDashboard balance={2000} leverage={100} lots={0.5} move={0} caption="Increase the trade size with no price movement at all. Used margin rises, free margin falls, and the account gets more fragile without a single pip going against you." />

<Callout kind="warning" title="The overtrading trap">
Opening several positions at once feels like diversification. Often it is not: EUR/USD, GBP/USD and AUD/USD all long is essentially one big bet against the dollar, in three pieces.

You have tripled the locked margin and taken on one concentrated risk. That is the worst of both.
</Callout>

## The two numbers it feeds

Used margin is one half of the two figures that decide everything:

- **Free margin** = Equity − Used margin
- **Margin level** = (Equity ÷ Used margin) × 100

Both are coming next.
