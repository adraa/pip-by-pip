Free margin is the honest answer to "how much room do I have?"

<Formula expression="Free margin = Equity − Used margin" />

<Worked>
Balance **$2,000**, one trade open needing **$500** of margin, currently showing **minus $200**.

Equity = 2,000 − 200 = **$1,800**
Used margin = **$500**
Free margin = 1,800 − 500 = **$1,300**

That $1,300 is your cushion. It is what the market can take from you before things start going wrong.
</Worked>

<Callout kind="warning" title="Just because you can does not mean you should">
Free margin of $1,300 means your platform will happily let you open several more trades. It is telling you what is permitted, not what is wise.

The question is never "can I open this?" It is "if this goes wrong, and the others go wrong at the same time, what is left?"
</Callout>

## Watching it shrink

Two different things eat free margin, and they can happen together:

1. **Opening more trades** — used margin rises, so free margin falls even with no price movement.
2. **Trades going against you** — equity falls, so free margin falls.

Do both at once, which is exactly what a trader does when adding to a losing position, and it disappears alarmingly fast.

## When it goes negative

Free margin can go below zero. It means your floating losses have eaten past your entire deposit for those trades. At that point your broker is no longer watching. It is acting.

That is the margin call, and it is two lessons away.
