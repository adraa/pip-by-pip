Currency futures came first. Spot forex is what almost everyone actually trades. Here is the honest comparison.

## What a future is

A **futures contract** is an agreement to exchange a set amount of currency on a set date, traded on an exchange such as the CME in Chicago. Standardised size, standardised expiry date, one central marketplace.

Spot forex, by contrast, is an over-the-counter arrangement with your broker, with no expiry and any size you like.

## The comparison

| | Spot forex | Currency futures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where it trades | Over the counter, via your broker | A central exchange |
| Price | Varies slightly between brokers | One official price for everyone |
| Volume data | Not real; your broker's only | Genuine, published |
| Hours | 24 hours, 5 days | Exchange hours, with an evening break |
| Minimum size | Micro lot, 1,000 units | One contract, often 125,000 units |
| Expiry | None | Fixed dates, must close or roll |
| Costs | Spread | Commission plus exchange fees |
| Counterparty | Your broker | The exchange clearing house |
| Regulation | Varies enormously by country | Strong, exchange-based |

## The two real advantages of futures

<Callout title="One price, and real volume">
Because everything trades in one place, there is a single official price nobody can shade, and the exchange publishes genuine volume figures.

Spot forex has neither. When an indicator on your platform shows "volume", it is your broker's tick count, not the market's. This is a bigger deal than it first appears, and it is why some technical tools work less reliably in forex than the textbooks suggest.
</Callout>

For most people learning with a small account, the size and flexibility of spot decide it. That is a perfectly reasonable choice, made with open eyes.

<Callout kind="warning">
Just note what you are giving up. In spot forex your broker is often the other side of your trade and there is no exchange watching. That is exactly why the Kindergarten lessons on choosing a properly regulated broker matter as much as they do.
</Callout>

## One thing worth borrowing

Even committed spot traders often watch futures data, particularly the weekly **Commitments of Traders** report, which shows how big institutions are positioned. It is real data about a market with no real data of its own. You will meet it properly in Undergraduate.
