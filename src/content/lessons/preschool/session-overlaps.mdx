Two sessions open at once means twice the participants, and it changes the character of the market completely.

<SessionsClock caption="Look for the two moments when bars light up together: around 07:00 to 09:00, and 12:00 to 16:00 GMT." />

## The main event: London and New York

**12:00 to 16:00 GMT.** Four hours in which the two biggest centres on earth trade simultaneously.

- The **tightest spreads** of the day, so every trade is cheaper
- The **largest moves**, so a good idea actually has room to pay
- **US economic releases** land right in the middle of it
- Deep liquidity, so orders fill where you expect them to

If you have limited time, put it here. It is not a close call.

## The secondary one: Tokyo and London

**07:00 to 09:00 GMT.** Smaller, but genuinely useful. Europe arrives while Asia is still finishing, and the quiet Asian range typically breaks in this window. Traders who like breakouts often work here.

## Why quiet hours are worse than they look

The obvious cost of a thin market is the wider spread. The subtler cost is that **nothing happens**.

<Worked title="The same trade, two different hours">
You want 20 pips. The spread costs you 1 pip in the overlap and 3.5 pips at 3am.

**In the overlap:** the pair routinely moves 20 pips in an hour. You need 21 pips of movement to make your target. Realistic.

**At 3am:** the pair might move 20 pips in six hours. You need 23.5 pips to make the same target. You will probably still be sitting there at breakfast.

Same idea, same market, same trader. The hour did all the damage.
</Worked>

<Callout kind="warning" title="The overlap is not free money">
Everything is faster here. A sloppy entry that would drift harmlessly at 3am can be 30 pips against you in two minutes at 14:00. The window rewards a plan and punishes improvisation.
</Callout>

## Building a routine around it

If you work a normal job, one of these is probably possible:

- **Trade the London open**, 07:00 to 09:00 GMT, before work if you are in Europe or Asia.
- **Trade the overlap**, 12:00 to 16:00 GMT, if your afternoons are free or you are in the Americas.
- **Place orders in advance** and let them work while you are busy. Perfectly legitimate, and it removes the temptation to fiddle.

What is not a good plan is trading whenever you happen to be free, with no thought about which hours those are. The hour you choose changes the market you are trading.
