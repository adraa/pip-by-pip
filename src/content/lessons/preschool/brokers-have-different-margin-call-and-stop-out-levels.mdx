The last two lessons used 100% and 50%. Those are common defaults, not universal law. Your broker may be entirely different, and the difference matters.

## The range you will actually find

| Setup | Margin call | Stop out | What it feels like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Combined | 100% | 100% | No warning zone at all |
| Common | 100% | 50% | A useful gap to react in |
| Generous | 100% | 20% | A long rope, for better and worse |
| Tight | 150% | 100% | Closed out early, which can be protective |
| Tiered | Varies by size | Varies by size | Rules change as your position grows |

<Callout kind="warning" title="Lower is not safer">
A 20% stop out sounds generous. It means your broker lets the position run until 80% of your equity relative to margin is gone.

Generous and dangerous are the same thing here. More rope keeps a bad trade alive longer, and it is only good news if you were going to act yourself, which you were not, or you would have already.
</Callout>

## The other rules nobody reads

Beyond the two headline numbers, brokers apply conditions that surprise people at bad moments.

## Three questions to ask, today

Send these to your broker's support and keep the reply.

1. What are my margin call and stop out levels, as percentages?
2. Do margin requirements change with position size, on Fridays, or around news?
3. Do you offer negative balance protection?

<Callout>
Any legitimate broker answers all three plainly within a day. Vagueness, deflection or a link to a fifty-page PDF with no page number is itself the answer.
</Callout>

## Why this is worth ten minutes now

You will not think about any of this while a trade is going well. You will only think about it in the middle of a bad afternoon, and by then it will be too late to find out.

Ten minutes today. It is the cheapest risk management available.
