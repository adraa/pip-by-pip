## The whole thing in one rule

**Limits wait for a better price. Stops trigger at a worse one.**

Everything else follows from that, including which side of the current price the order sits on.

| Order         | Sits        | You are saying                                   | Typical use              |
| ------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------ |
| Buy market    | Right here  | Get me in now                                     | Immediate entry          |
| Sell market   | Right here  | Get me out now, or short now                      | Immediate exit or entry  |
| **Buy limit** | **Below**   | Buy, but only if it gets cheaper                  | Buying a dip             |
| **Sell limit**| **Above**   | Sell, but only if it gets more expensive          | Selling a rally          |
| **Buy stop**  | **Above**   | Buy once it breaks upward through this level      | Upside breakout          |
| **Sell stop** | **Below**   | Sell once it breaks downward through this level   | Downside breakout, or a stop loss on a long |

<Callout title="The memory trick">
Limit orders are optimistic: they wait for a bargain. Stop orders are defensive or impatient: they accept a worse price to get in or out. Optimists sit on the good side of the price, realists on the bad side.
</Callout>

## The exits

- **Stop loss** — a stop order attached to an open trade, closing it if you are wrong. On a long it is a sell stop below your entry. On a short it is a buy stop above.
- **Take profit** — a limit order attached to an open trade, closing it at your target. On a long it is a sell limit above your entry.

Look at that carefully. Your stop loss and your take profit are just the same two order types you already know, pointed in opposite directions.

<Callout kind="warning">
Every platform lays this out slightly differently, and mixing up a stop and a limit is a genuinely expensive mistake. Place your first few orders on a demo account and check that the little line appears on the side of the price you expected.
</Callout>
