## The Friday afternoon problem

From roughly 16:00 GMT on a Friday, a great deal of the movement you see is not an opinion about anything. It is **position squaring**: traders closing out so they are not exposed over the weekend.

<Callout kind="warning">
A Friday afternoon move can look exactly like the start of a trend and mean nothing at all. It frequently reverses on Monday. Reading it as a signal is a common and expensive mistake.
</Callout>

## Weekend risk is real

The market closes; the world does not. Elections, central bank statements, conflicts and disasters all happen at weekends, and everything gets priced in at once when trading resumes on Sunday evening.

<Worked title="Why the gap matters">
You are long EUR/USD at **1.0850**, with a stop loss at **1.0800**, holding over the weekend.

Serious news breaks on Saturday. The market reopens on Sunday at **1.0700**.

Your stop was at 1.0800, but the price never traded there. It jumped straight past. Your stop becomes a market order and fills around 1.0700.

You planned to lose 50 pips. You lost 150. The stop worked exactly as designed and still did not save you.
</Worked>

That is the one genuine reason to think hard about Friday. Not superstition about days, just the plain fact that a closed market cannot be exited.

## A reasonable weekly rhythm

- **Monday:** read, plan, mostly watch.
- **Tuesday to Thursday:** trade properly. This is the week.
- **Friday morning:** trade if there is something worth trading.
- **Friday afternoon:** close up, write the journal, stop.

Not trading is a position. It is often the correct one.
