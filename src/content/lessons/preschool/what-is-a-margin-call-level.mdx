A margin call is your broker tapping you on the shoulder and saying: *we are both getting uncomfortable now.*

## The two words mean different things

The **margin call level** is a number set by your broker, usually 100%. The **margin call** is what happens when your margin level touches it.

<Formula expression="Margin call level = the margin level % at which the broker intervenes" note="Most commonly 100%. Check yours; they vary." />

## What 100% actually means

<Worked>
Balance **$1,000**, one mini lot open, used margin **$108**.

The trade goes against you by **$892**.

Equity = 1,000 − 892 = **$108**
Used margin = **$108**
Margin level = (108 ÷ 108) × 100 = **100%**

Your entire account is now doing nothing but holding the deposit on one trade. There is no cushion left at all. Free margin is exactly zero.
</Worked>

<Callout kind="warning" title="It is not a request">
The name is a leftover from an era when a broker genuinely telephoned to ask for more money. Nothing is being asked now. It is an automatic warning from a system that is already preparing to close your trades.
</Callout>

## What to do about it

Honestly? By the time you are here, all the good options are behind you.

## The real lesson

A margin call is not a market event. It is the arithmetic result of trading too large for the account. Nobody who risks 1% of their equity on a trade ever sees one.

If you have hit a margin call, the size was wrong. That is the finding, and it is worth more than the money it cost.
