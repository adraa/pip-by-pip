Everything in this section has been leading here, and the conclusion is unglamorous: **you avoid margin calls by trading smaller.**

That is not one technique among many. It is the whole answer, and everything below is a way of implementing it.

## Sizing, worked properly

<Worked title="The calculation, every time">
Account equity: **$1,000**
Risk per trade at 1%: **$10**
Your analysis puts a sensible stop **25 pips** away.

Value per pip you can afford = $10 ÷ 25 = **$0.40 per pip**

A micro lot is $0.10 per pip, so you can trade **4 micro lots**, or 0.04 lots.

Now check it: if the stop is hit you lose $10, exactly as planned. Margin used is about $43. Your margin level stays in the hundreds of percent throughout. A margin call is simply not on the table.
</Worked>

Do that arithmetic before every trade and this entire section becomes theoretical knowledge, which is exactly where you want it.

## The uncomfortable truth

<Callout kind="warning">
A margin call is not something that happens to you. It is something you set up in advance, by choosing a size, and then wait for the market to deliver.

Nobody who risks 1% per trade with a stop loss has ever received one. Not because they were lucky, or right, but because the arithmetic does not allow it.
</Callout>
