Every trade you read about online will be described in slang. None of it is difficult; it is just unfamiliar. Here is the whole vocabulary in one sitting.

## Pair nicknames

| Nickname | Pair    | Where it comes from                                          |
| -------- | ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Cable    | GBP/USD | The transatlantic telegraph cable that carried the rate in the 1800s |
| Fiber    | EUR/USD | A modern joke on cable: the euro is newer, so fibre optic     |
| Loonie   | USD/CAD | The loon bird on the Canadian dollar coin                     |
| Aussie   | AUD/USD | Australia                                                     |
| Kiwi     | NZD/USD | The bird on the New Zealand dollar coin                       |
| Swissy   | USD/CHF | Switzerland                                                   |
| Gopher   | USD/JPY | Trader shorthand, no elegant origin                           |
| Guppy    | GBP/JPY | GBP plus JPY, said quickly                                    |

<Callout>
When a nickname is used on its own, it means that currency against the US dollar. "The Aussie is rising" means AUD/USD is going up, not AUD/JPY.
</Callout>

## The rest of it

- **Long** — you bought. **Short** — you sold.
- **Flat** or **square** — no open trades at all.
- **Stopped out** — your stop loss triggered and the trade closed at a loss.
- **Blown up** — the account is gone. You will read this a lot.
- **Choppy** — the price is going nowhere in an annoying, whipsawing way.
- **Risk-on** — traders feel brave, so money moves into the Aussie and Kiwi.
- **Risk-off** — traders feel scared, so money runs to the yen, the franc and the dollar.
- **Handle** or **big figure** — the whole-number part of the price. At 1.0850 the handle is "one oh eight".
- **Bagholder** — someone still clinging to a losing trade, hoping.
- **Whipsaw** — the price stops you out and then immediately goes the way you originally wanted. Deeply personal.

## Why bother

Two reasons. You will read charts, forums and analysis written entirely in this dialect, and translating it in your head is exhausting until it becomes automatic.

And the second: this vocabulary is the *whole* of what separates a "professional-sounding" trader from a beginner. Not skill, not results. Just words. Which is a useful thing to know when you are deciding who to listen to.
