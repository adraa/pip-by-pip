Yes, you can. Brokers will happily open an account for $100 or less. The interesting question is what happens next.

## What $100 can actually do

<Worked title="One micro lot, sensibly">
Account: **$100**, leverage 100:1.
Trade: **one micro lot** (1,000 units) of EUR/USD.

Required margin: about **$10.85**
One pip: **$0.10**

A 50 pip win: **+$5.00**
A 30 pip loss: **−$3.00**

Perfectly survivable. You could be wrong thirty times in a row and still have an account.
</Worked>

That is real trading. Real money, real mechanics, real feelings, at a cost of a few dollars a week while you learn.

## What $100 cannot do

The same maths, read the other way, is the problem.

<Callout kind="warning" title="The boredom trap">
A good month on that account might make **$15**. That is a genuinely excellent 15% return, and it is fifteen dollars.

Fifteen dollars is not exciting. So the trader thinks: what if I traded a mini lot instead, ten times bigger? Now the same month makes $150.

And a 50 pip loss, which happens constantly, is $50. Half the account. Two of those and it is over.
</Callout>

This is why small accounts die. Not the market. The arithmetic of impatience.

## The mini lot on $100, in numbers

<Worked title="What ten times too big looks like">
Account **$100**, one **mini lot** of EUR/USD. One pip is **$1**.
Required margin: **$108.50**.

The margin needed is more than the entire account. The trade will be rejected.

At the highest offshore leverage the trade might squeeze through, and then a **90 pip** move, an ordinary Tuesday, closes the account entirely.
</Worked>

## The honest way to use a small account

<Steps
  steps={[
    { title: 'Decide it is tuition, not capital', body: 'This $100 is what you are paying to learn. If it goes, you learned something. Do not deposit money you need.' },
    { title: 'Trade micro lots only', body: 'Ten cents a pip. If your broker offers nano lots, better still.' },
    { title: 'Risk about 1% per trade', body: 'One dollar. With a micro lot that is roughly a 10 pip stop, which is tight but workable on the right pairs.' },
    { title: 'Measure in pips, not money', body: 'A 60 pip month is a 60 pip month whether the account is $100 or $100,000. Judge the skill, not the amount.' },
    { title: 'Add funds from income, not from winning', body: 'Grow the account by depositing from your salary as your skill improves, not by trading larger to catch up.' },
  ]}
/>

<Callout>
Here is the reframe that makes a small account work. You are not trying to turn $100 into $1,000. You are trying to prove, cheaply, that you can follow a plan for six months.

If you can do that, the account size becomes a solvable problem. If you cannot, no account size would have saved you.
</Callout>
