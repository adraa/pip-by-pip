Tokyo is the first proper session of the trading day, and it has a distinct personality: **contained**.

<SessionsClock caption="Set the slider to around 03:00 GMT. Tokyo is open, everyone else is asleep, and that is the whole Asian session in one picture." />

## What it feels like

Prices during Tokyo hours tend to pick a ceiling and a floor and bounce gently between them. Big directional runs are the exception, not the rule.

There is a straightforward reason. The volume that produces big moves lives in London and New York. During Tokyo hours it is simply not there, so the price has nothing pushing it hard in one direction.

## Which pairs actually move

Local business drives local currencies, so during these hours the action is in:

- **USD/JPY** and the other yen pairs, since this is Japan's working day
- **AUD/USD** and **NZD/USD**, since Australia and New Zealand are awake
- **AUD/JPY** and **NZD/JPY**, which combine both

Meanwhile **EUR/GBP** or **EUR/CHF** are barely breathing. Europe is asleep, and nobody with a reason to move those pairs is at their desk.

<Callout>
Trade what is awake. Trading a European cross during Tokyo hours means paying a wider spread for a price that is barely moving. It is the worst of both.
</Callout>

## The interesting hour

From about **07:00 GMT** London traders start arriving while Tokyo is still open. Volume jumps, and the tidy Asian range frequently breaks.

Plenty of traders never trade the Asian session at all. They just note where the range formed and use those levels as their map for the London open. That is a perfectly respectable way to use these hours, and it involves no trading whatsoever.
