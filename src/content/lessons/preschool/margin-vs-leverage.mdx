These two words get used interchangeably, and they describe the same relationship from opposite ends.

<Formula expression="Leverage = 100 ÷ Margin requirement %" note="100:1 leverage and a 1% margin requirement are the same sentence." />

| Leverage | Margin requirement | To control $100,000 you need |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 500:1 | 0.2% | $200 |
| 100:1 | 1% | $1,000 |
| 50:1 | 2% | $2,000 |
| 30:1 | 3.33% | $3,333 |
| 10:1 | 10% | $10,000 |

<Analogy>
A shop offers a "10% deposit" scheme. Another offers "ten times your money in credit". These are the same offer, described by a cautious accountant and an enthusiastic salesman.

Margin is the accountant's phrasing. Leverage is the salesman's. That is genuinely the whole difference.
</Analogy>

## The number that actually matters

Here is the distinction worth carrying away. Your broker's leverage is a **ceiling**. What matters is how much of it you use.

<Formula expression="Effective leverage = Total position size ÷ Equity" />

<Worked title="Two traders, same broker, same 500:1 available">
**Trader A**: $10,000 equity, one micro lot open ($1,085).
Effective leverage: about **0.1:1**. Practically unleveraged.

**Trader B**: $1,000 equity, three standard lots open ($325,500).
Effective leverage: about **325:1**. A 30 pip move ends them.

Same broker. Same offer. Two entirely different activities.
</Worked>

<Callout kind="warning" title="This is the whole point of the margin section">
"How much leverage does my broker offer?" is almost the wrong question. "How much am I using?" is the right one.

You can be reckless at 20:1 and conservative at 500:1. The setting on the account does not decide; the size of your trades does.
</Callout>

## Sensible effective leverage

Rough guidance for someone learning:

- **Under 5:1** — conservative, comfortable, and where you should live while learning.
- **5:1 to 10:1** — active trading with proper risk control.
- **10:1 to 20:1** — aggressive. Only with a tested plan and tight stops.
- **Above 20:1** — you are relying on being right quickly.

<Worked title="Work out yours">
Equity: **$2,000**. Open: **2 mini lots** of EUR/USD at 1.0850, so $21,700 of position.

21,700 ÷ 2,000 = **10.85:1**

Aggressive, but within reach of sane. Add a third mini lot and you are at 16:1, and the question becomes whether your stop losses are tight enough to justify it.
</Worked>

Check this number occasionally. It takes ten seconds and it is a far better health indicator than your profit and loss.
