There is never one price. There are always two: one to buy at, one to sell at. The gap between them is the **spread**, and it is how most forex brokers get paid.

<Figure caption="Two prices, always. You buy at the higher one and sell at the lower one, whichever direction you trade.">
  <SpreadBoard pair="EUR/USD" bid="1.08500" ask="1.08512" spread="1.2 pips" />
</Figure>

<Analogy>
The money changer at the airport has a board with two columns. They will sell you dollars at 84 rupees and buy them back at 82. Walk up, change rupees to dollars and immediately change back, and you are poorer by two rupees per dollar without anything happening in the world.

That is the spread. Forex spreads are much smaller, but the mechanism is identical.
</Analogy>

## You start every trade slightly behind

This is the part worth internalising. The moment you open a trade, it is already losing, by exactly the spread.

<Worked>
EUR/USD, spread of 1.2 pips. You buy at the ask of **1.08512**.

The screen immediately shows you down **1.2 pips**, because to close you would have to sell at the bid of 1.08500.

The market has not moved at all. You simply crossed the spread.

For the trade to break even, the price has to rise 1.2 pips. To make 10 pips of actual profit, it has to rise 11.2.
</Worked>

<Callout kind="warning" title="The 3am tax">
The same pair can cost 0.8 pips at 2pm London and 4 pips at 3am. If you are trading in the quiet hours because that is when you are free, check the spread before you enter, every time. It is a real and often overlooked cost.
</Callout>

## Does it matter much?

It depends entirely on how far you are trying to go.

- Aiming for **5 pips** with a **1.5 pip** spread: you are giving away 30% of your target before you begin. Brutal.
- Aiming for **200 pips** with the same spread: you are giving away 0.75%. Barely worth thinking about.

Fast trading and wide spreads are a bad marriage. That single fact quietly rules out a whole style of trading for people with expensive brokers, and it is worth knowing before you fall in love with scalping.
