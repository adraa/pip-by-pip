The honest answer is yes, and the honest answer is also that almost nobody does.

Both of those are true at once, and holding both is the healthiest thing you can do at this stage.

## The number the industry has to publish

Regulators in Europe, the UK and Australia require brokers to display what proportion of their retail customers lose money. Go and look at the small print on any broker's homepage.

<Figure caption="A typical published figure from a regulated broker's own disclosure. Brokers are not incentivised to make this look bad.">
  <BarCompare
    unit="% of accounts"
    items={[
      { label: 'Lose money', value: 76, highlight: true },
      { label: 'Make money', value: 24 },
    ]}
  />
</Figure>

Roughly three in four. Not from a critic, from the brokers themselves, under legal obligation.

## Why the internet tells you otherwise

<Callout kind="warning" title="Survivorship bias">
Nobody posts a screenshot of the account they destroyed. The person who lost their savings deletes the app and never mentions it again. What is left online is the small surviving fraction, plus a large population of people selling courses.

You are not seeing a sample of traders. You are seeing a sample of people who have a reason to be visible.
</Callout>

And there is a second layer: many of the loudest "traders" make their money selling signals, courses and referral links, not from trading. Their income depends on your optimism, not on their results.

## The arithmetic of a small account

Suppose you are genuinely good. Say you make 5% a month, which is an excellent, professional-grade return.

<Worked>
Starting with **$500**, at 5% per month:

- After 1 year: about **$897**
- After 3 years: about **$2,885**
- After 5 years: about **$9,280**

Five years of doing something difficult extremely well, and you have nine thousand dollars. Meanwhile you were tempted, every single month, to make it faster.
</Worked>

That temptation is the killer. Not the market, the impatience. The mathematics of a small account push you toward oversized risk, and oversized risk is what actually empties accounts.

<Callout>
The people who do make serious money from trading are mostly doing it with serious capital, or trading other people's. Skill is only half the equation. The other half is how much money the skill is applied to.
</Callout>

None of that means do not do it. It means do it with your eyes open, with money you can genuinely afford to lose, and while keeping your income.

Set the goal for year one as **"finish the year having followed my plan, without doing anything catastrophic."** That is a demanding target, and hitting it puts you ahead of most people who ever open an account.
