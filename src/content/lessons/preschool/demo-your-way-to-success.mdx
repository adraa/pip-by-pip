Nobody's first fifty trades are any good. That is not pessimism, it is arithmetic: you are learning a skill, and the early attempts at any skill are bad.

The only question is whether those first fifty trades cost you money.

## What demo is actually for

Not for proving you can trade. For getting the boring mistakes out of the way.

## What demo cannot teach you

<Callout kind="warning" title="The honest limitation">
A demo account cannot teach you how you behave when real money is disappearing. Fake losses produce fake feelings. Traders who are calm and disciplined on demo routinely fall apart in their first live week, and they are always surprised.
</Callout>

That is not a reason to skip demo. It is a reason not to over-trust it, and a reason to make the step to live money as small as possible.

## The sensible path

<Steps
  steps={[
    { title: 'Weeks on demo', body: 'Until placing a trade with a stop loss is as automatic as sending a message.' },
    { title: 'Write the plan down', body: 'What you trade, when you enter, where the stop goes, how big the trade is. On paper.' },
    { title: 'Follow it on demo', body: 'For long enough to have a real sample. Not three trades. Dozens.' },
    { title: 'Go live in micro lots', body: 'Ten cents a pip. Small enough that a bad week costs less than a meal out.' },
    { title: 'Grow slowly, if at all', body: 'Size up only when the same plan keeps working and you have stopped feeling your heartbeat.' },
  ]}

<Analogy>
Nobody learns to drive by buying a car and setting off down the motorway. They sit in an empty car park until the pedals stop requiring thought.

Demo is the empty car park. It is not driving. It is the part that makes driving survivable.
</Analogy>

## One warning about demo success

Demo accounts often hand you a fake $100,000. Trading a hundred thousand dollars teaches you nothing about trading the amount you will actually deposit.

If you plan to start with $500, set your demo balance to $500. A demo triumph made with money you will never have is not practice. It is a video game.
